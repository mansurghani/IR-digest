Transarterial Radioembolization Versus Chemoembolization for Unresectable Large Single Hepatocellular Carcinoma.

Korean J Radiol. 2026 Sep 14;27(10):961–72. doi: 10.3348/kjr.2026.0458

Read the original articlePubMed

Overview

This single-center retrospective study from Seoul National University Hospital compared TARE (n=119) with TACE (n=187) as initial therapy for unresectable solitary HCC ≥6 cm without vascular invasion. After IPTW, TARE was associated with longer median OS (79.6 vs 53.4 months, P = 0.033) and local PFS (25.1 vs 9.5 months, P = 0.001), but the difference in overall PFS was not significant. TARE had fewer SAEs. A key feature is that 39 TACE patients were TARE-intended but ineligible because of high lung dose. They did very poorly (median OS 20.3 months), so the TARE advantage may partly reflect selection of favorable tumor biology.

Study design

Retrospective single-center cohort. Patients treated January 2010 to December 2023. Consecutive eligible patients, n=306 (187 TACE, 119 TARE). No randomization or blinding. Primary outcome OS. Secondary outcomes were PFS, local PFS, best mRECIST response and safety (CTCAE v5.0 grade ≥3 within 90 days). Analysis used Kaplan-Meier with log-rank tests. Confounding was handled with IPTW, with covariates chosen by SMD >0.2, and with a multivariable Cox model as sensitivity analysis. Survival data came from hospital records and a national registry.

Patients

Inclusion criteria were TACE or TARE as the initial treatment, a single tumor ≥6 cm, age ≥18, ECOG ≤1, and Child-Pugh A or B7. Exclusions were prior resection, ablation or systemic therapy; vascular invasion or extrahepatic metastasis; and other malignancy within 2 years. Median age was 70 years in both arms; 157/187 TACE and 98/119 TARE patients were men. Before weighting, TARE patients had better liver function and less aggressive markers: albumin ≥4 g/dL in 57.1% vs 39.0%, ALBI grade 1 in 64.7% vs 47.1%, AFP ≥200 in 25.2% vs 42.8%, and tumor >80 mm in 68.9% vs 60.4%. The TACE arm included 39 patients (20.9%) who were planned for TARE but had a high estimated lung dose. Of the 187 TACE patients, 153 had cTACE and 34 had DEB-TACE.

Intervention and comparator

TACE was done by eight IRs, as cTACE (doxorubicin up to 50 mg, Lipiodol up to 10 mL, gelatin sponge) or DEB-TACE (DC Bead 100-300 or 300-500 µm, 25-50 mg doxorubicin per vial, 1-2 vials). It was repeated, apparently until radiologic response. TARE was done by three IRs with Y-90 glass (75.6%) or resin (24.4%) microspheres. Delivery was as selective as feasible, often with a boosted dose, and included extrahepatic collateral feeders. Median vessels treated was 3, and most treatment was segmental or subsegmental. Dosimetry was retrospective (Simplicit90Y). Median LSF was 4.1%. Median mAD was 231 Gy (IQR 169-318), pre-treatment TAD on SPECT/CT was 416 Gy, and post-treatment TAD on PET/CT was 582 Gy (IQR 374-778). Median tumor volume was 366 mL.
